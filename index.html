EVERY STORY HAS A RECORD.

This index brings together the research and references used throughout BLACKNWHITE. It is the central reference list for the Movement, History, Impact, Youth and Evidence pages.

The sources include historical information, court decisions, legislation, organisations, photographs, government records and examples of young people taking action. Each section explains which parts of the website its sources help support.

These references allow readers to check information, investigate events further and distinguish between historical evidence, research and modern examples. Whenever you want to check where an idea or fact came from, start here.

03 / THE RESEARCH INDEX

FOLLOW THE SOURCES.

This research index is organised around the five main content pages of BLACKNWHITE. Each section identifies the sources that support the information, examples and analysis on that page. Some references appear more than once because they provide evidence for different parts of the project.

THE MOVEMENT

01 / PEOPLE & ORGANISATIONS

These sources support the Movement page's discussion of the people, leaders, organisations and campaigns involved in challenging racial segregation in the United States. They provide historical background on groups such as the NAACP, SCLC, SNCC and CORE, as well as individuals and events that helped shape the movement.

  • Library of Congress — The NAACP and the Civil Rights Era

    Provides historical context about the NAACP's role in legal challenges, campaigns and the wider Civil Rights Movement.

  • SNCC Digital Gateway — Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee

    Explains the work of student activists, organisers and local communities, including the development of SNCC.

  • Library of Congress — Civil Rights History Project

    Contains interviews and historical material from people who participated in the movement.

  • Library of Congress — Voices of the Civil Rights Movement

    Provides first-hand accounts and perspectives from people involved in civil rights activism.

  • Library of Congress — James Meredith photograph record

    Supports the page's visual and historical references to James Meredith and school desegregation.

  • Library of Congress — Martin Luther King Jr. photograph record

    Provides an archival photograph record connected to one of the movement's most recognised leaders.

  • NAACP — Organisation and history

    Provides information about the organisation's history and its continuing work for civil rights.

  • National Park Service — Modern Civil Rights Movement

    Provides a broader historical overview of major campaigns, people and events.

HISTORY

02 / BACKGROUND & DEVELOPMENT

These sources support the History page's explanation of segregation, the emergence of the movement, major events and the historical conditions that led people to organise. They also provide information about court decisions and the development of civil rights campaigns over time.

  • National Archives — Brown v. Board of Education timeline

    Explains the legal challenge to school segregation and the events surrounding the Supreme Court decision.

  • Library of Congress — Civil Rights Movement timeline

    Places important civil rights events within the wider history of the United States after World War II.

  • Library of Congress — Civil Rights Era timeline

    Provides a timeline of key events and turning points during the Civil Rights Era.

  • Library of Congress — Civil Rights Movement primary source set

    Includes historical documents and records that help explain the movement through original evidence.

  • Encyclopaedia Britannica — American Civil Rights Movement

    Provides a general historical overview of the movement, its causes, major events and outcomes.

  • Library of Congress — African American Odyssey: Civil Rights Era

    Explores the wider history of African American activism and the struggle for civil rights.

  • Library of Congress — The NAACP and the Civil Rights Era

    Provides background on the campaigns and legal challenges that contributed to the movement's development.

THE IMPACT

03 / METHODS & SOCIAL CHANGE

These sources support the Impact page's analysis of protest methods, nonviolent action, legal achievements and the limitations of social change. They help explain what the movement achieved, how those changes occurred and why discrimination did not disappear after new laws were passed.

  • National Archives — Civil Rights Act of 1964

    Provides the historical document and background for the legislation that prohibited discrimination in several areas of public life.

  • National Archives — Voting Rights Act of 1965

    Supports discussion of federal action against discriminatory voting practices.

  • Library of Congress — Nonviolent Philosophy and Self Defense

    Explains ideas and debates around nonviolent protest and self-defence during the movement.

  • Library of Congress — Civil Rights Movement primary source set

    Provides historical documents and examples that can be used to examine protest methods and their effects.

  • National Park Service — Modern Civil Rights Movement

    Provides context for the movement's achievements and the ongoing struggle for civil rights.

  • U.S. Census Bureau — Poverty data tables

    Provides statistical information that can help examine wider social and economic inequalities.

  • U.S. Census Bureau — Income and poverty information

    Provides background information and data about income and poverty in the United States.

YOUNG PEOPLE & CHANGE

04 / THE YOUTH PAGE

These sources support the Youth page's investigation of young people's involvement in the American Civil Rights Movement. They cover the Little Rock Nine, student sit-ins, the Birmingham Children's Crusade and the role of student organisers. The final sources are used separately for the modern Australian student protest example.

  • National Park Service — The Little Rock Nine

    Explains how nine Black teenagers challenged school segregation at Central High School in Little Rock, Arkansas, in 1957.

  • SNCC Digital Gateway — Student activism and organising

    Provides historical information about student-led organising, direct action and the role of young activists in the movement.

  • Library of Congress — The Civil Rights Era

    Provides context for the wider movement and the campaigns in which young people participated.

  • National Archives — African American Civil Rights records

    Provides access to government records and historical material connected to the struggle for civil rights.

  • Library of Congress — Civil Rights Movement primary source set

    Offers historical documents and materials that can support research into student activism and civil rights campaigns.

  • ABC News — Students protest Pauline Hanson, August 2026

    Reports on a modern Australian student protest. This is used as a contemporary example of young people participating in public demonstrations, not as part of the historical American movement.

  • Australian Associated Press — Second wave of student protests, September 2026

    Provides additional reporting on student demonstrations in Australia and helps examine youth participation in current social issues.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION: The American Civil Rights Movement is the main historical case study on this page. The Australian student protests are included as a separate modern example to show how young people can participate in social movements today. They are not presented as part of the American Civil Rights Movement.

THE EVIDENCE

05 / PRIMARY RECORDS

These sources support the Evidence page's use of photographs, archival records, government documents and historical collections. Primary sources are especially important because they allow readers to examine records created during the period, rather than relying only on later descriptions of events.

  • Library of Congress — Rosa Parks fingerprinting photograph

    An archival photograph record connected to Rosa Parks and the events surrounding the Montgomery Bus Boycott.

  • Library of Congress — Brown v. Board historical photograph

    Provides a visual record connected to the legal challenge against school segregation.

  • Library of Congress — 1963 March on Washington photograph

    Provides an archival record of the large demonstration held in Washington, D.C.

  • Library of Congress — Selma to Montgomery March photograph

    Documents the voting rights marches in Alabama in 1965.

  • National Archives — Civil Rights Act of 1964

    Provides access to the original legislation and information about its historical significance.

  • National Archives — Voting Rights Act of 1965

    Provides access to the legislation that addressed discriminatory voting practices.

  • Library of Congress — Selma to Montgomery March collection

    Contains photographs and other historical materials relating to the 1965 voting rights marches.

  • Library of Congress — Civil Rights History Project collection

    Provides interviews and historical material that preserve the experiences of people involved in the movement.

  • National Archives — African American Civil Rights records

    Provides access to government records and other primary sources relating to the struggle for civil rights.

  • Wikimedia Commons — Historical photograph archive

    Provides access to historical images. Individual image records should be checked for their original source, creator and usage information.

RESEARCH NOTE: This index gathers the main sources referenced across BLACKNWHITE. Sources are grouped according to the page and topic they support. Some references appear in more than one section because the same historical document or collection can provide evidence for different parts of the project.

Primary sources, such as photographs, government documents and first-hand accounts, are used alongside secondary sources that provide historical explanations and context. Photographs should be credited to their individual record pages wherever possible, rather than only to the wider collection.
